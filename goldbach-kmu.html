Goldbach KMU Website

A redesign of Goldbach's 24-page KMU website section, combining research, CMS-based layouts and ongoing optimisation, alongside separate UX feedback work on Goldbach's B2B advertising-booking platform.

Company
Goldbach
Role
Product, UX & Motion
Scope
24-page KMU website section

Responsive presentation of the redesigned Goldbach KMU website.

Context and challenge

Goldbach's KMU section — 24 subpages aimed at small and medium businesses — needed a redesign that made the content easier to navigate and easier to maintain through the CMS. The work also had to hold up under real usage data, not just look right at launch.

Separately, Goldbach's multichannel B2B advertising-booking platform needed practical UX feedback grounded in real testing, and its users needed clearer guidance on how to use it.

My role and collaboration

I worked as multimedia designer on product design, UX and motion for the KMU website. My work included user research, Hotjar heatmap analysis, wireframes, CMS layouts, new website elements, page content, visual assets and icons, and I worked with developers on functional and A/B testing through to launch.

Separately, I tested new functions of the B2B platform and gave developers concrete UX feedback, and created tutorial videos and user guides to support it.

Approach

  1. Analysed Hotjar heatmaps and the existing page structure to identify where users lost orientation across the 24 subpages.

  2. Simplified the information architecture and built wireframes and CMS-based page layouts from it.

  3. Produced new website elements, content, visual assets and icons, then worked with developers on functional and A/B testing before launch.

  4. Continued optimising the section after launch based on ongoing results.

Image 02 goldbach-kmu-research-structure.jpg

Hotjar insight, simplified information architecture and representative wireframes.

Key design decisions

Reusable CMS modules instead of one-off page designs

With 24 subpages to maintain, reusable modules kept the section consistent and made future edits realistic without redesigning each page individually.

Heatmap-informed structure over assumption-based layout

Hotjar data showed where users actually engaged and where they dropped off, so structural decisions were grounded in observed behaviour rather than guesswork.

Process diagrams and icons to explain the B2B platform on the website

Rather than leaving platform education to support requests, integrating clear visual guidance let users understand the platform's process without needing to ask.

The solution

The redesigned KMU section runs on reusable CMS modules — consistent headers, content blocks, icons and page patterns — so each of the 24 subpages stays visually aligned while remaining editable without a full redesign.

Platform-related content, such as process explanations and tutorial material, was integrated into the website directly using diagrams and icons, so users could understand the B2B platform's workflow without leaving the page.

Outcome

Two separate parts of this work produced measurable results. They come from different activities and shouldn't be read as one combined effect.

+25% Generated leads, from continuous optimisation of the KMU website
+30% Time on page, after introducing B2B platform information and educational content

The educational content also reduced support demand for the B2B platform.

Reflection

Working across a website redesign and a separate platform-testing role at the same time made clear how much UX work outside a single flow is about explaining things well, whether that's a page layout or a tutorial video. Keeping the two strands of work distinct made it easier to see what was actually driving each result.
